## Where a page lives

**In the workspace** — for things that are not about one project: how the team works, the
on-call rota, the decision log.

**In a project** — for things that are. The project's Pages tab is the same wiki, scoped.

**Inside another page.** Pages nest to any depth. A page with children shows them at its foot and
in the sidebar tree, so a section is a page with pages under it rather than a separate kind of
object.

## Linking pages to each other

Type `[[` and the pages of the workspace are offered by title. Pick one and you get
`[[Onboarding]]`, which renders as a link to that page.

- **`[[Onboarding|how we start]]`** shows your words and links the page. A rename keeps them.
- **A link to a page nobody has written yet** is drawn faintly with a `+`. Following it creates
  that page, titled what you typed. This is how a wiki actually gets written: you say what is
  missing while you are writing about it, not later from a blank New page button.
- **The wiki index lists those**, under *Linked to, not written yet*, with how many pages want
  each one. It is the only to-do list here that writes itself.
- **Backlinks.** Every page shows what points at it, with the sentence the link was written in.
  Sub-pages are what a page contains; backlinks are what it is part of, and a wiki without them
  can only be read downwards.
- **Renaming carries the links.** Rename a page and every `[[…]]` that pointed at it is rewritten
  to follow, and the app says how many pages it touched. Each of those keeps its previous
  revision in its own [version history](/pages/history/).

Links resolve by title, so they work anywhere prose does — a chat message about the handbook
links to it the same way the handbook does. Two pages may share a title; the older one wins, so a
link that resolved yesterday still resolves to the same page today.

A [shared link](/pages/sharing/) carries the page and its children, so a `[[…]]` naming one of
those jumps to it inside the shared document. Anything else stays as written: a stranger following
a link into an app they cannot sign into is worse than seeing the brackets.

## Finding your way around

**The breadcrumb** above a page shows where it sits in the tree, from the top down. A page opened
from a search, a link or a bookmark used to say nothing at all about its place.

**Drag a page onto another** in the tree to nest it, or onto the gap between two to reorder. The
page menu has the same four moves for a keyboard — in, out, up, down.

## The editor

The same editor as comments and the chat composer, so everything here works in all three:

- **Markdown**, with a live preview of what you have typed.
- **`@mentions`** with autocomplete. Only a newly added handle notifies — see
  [comments and mentions](/tasks/comments/).
- **Task and project references.** `WEB-42` becomes a link; `#` offers projects and tasks.
- **Page links.** `[[` offers the pages of the workspace by title.
- **Images**, dragged or pasted. They downscale in your browser before upload.
- <kbd>Tab</kbd> indents by two spaces.
- **Mermaid diagrams** in a fenced ` ```mermaid ` block.

It saves as you type. There is no save button and no draft state, which is why the
[version history](/pages/history/) matters — it is the undo that survives closing the tab.

## Labels and filtering

Pages take labels the way tasks do, and the page list filters on them. On a wiki that has been
running for a year this is the difference between a tree somebody maintains and a tree nobody
can find anything in.

## Watching a page

**Watch** puts you on the list of people notified when it changes or is commented on. You are
watching automatically once you have written or commented on it.

## Page templates

*Settings → Templates & rules* holds page templates as well as task ones. A meeting note, a
decision record, an incident write-up — anything whose value is that everybody fills in the same
five headings.

## When a page beats a task description

A task description is for *what this task is*. A page is for anything that outlives the task:

| Put it in | |
|---|---|
| The task | Acceptance criteria, the reproduction steps, the link to the design |
| A page | The decision and why, the runbook, the thing the next person will search for |

The test is whether somebody would look for it after the task is closed. Closed tasks are
findable but nobody browses them, and a decision buried in `WEB-238` is a decision that gets
made again in March.
