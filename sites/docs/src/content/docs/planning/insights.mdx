*Project → Insights.* Everything on this tab is worked out from the tasks already on your
device. There is no aggregation table, no nightly job and nothing to wait for — which also means
it works on a train.

## The four numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Unfinished tasks, out of the project's total |
| **Finished** | In the last thirty days |
| **Typical time to finish** | The **median**, not the average — one task that sat in the backlog for a year would drag a mean somewhere nobody recognises |
| **Time logged** | See [time tracking](/planning/time/) |

## The four charts

**How much gets finished** — tasks reaching a done state, per week, for twelve weeks. Steady
beats spiky. A tall column after three empty ones usually does not mean a good week; it means
the work was finished long before somebody marked it.

**Burn-up** — two lines over the active cycle: everything in it, and everything finished. They
meet when the cycle is done. The scope line climbing mid-cycle is work being added, which is the
thing worth seeing.

**By label** — where the tasks are, in the project's own vocabulary. A task can wear several
labels, so the bars add up to more than the project has tasks.

**Open work per person** — unfinished tasks only. A task with two people on it counts for both,
which is why these numbers can exceed the total.

## How to read them without being misled

Three rules the charts follow, which are worth knowing because they are the opposite of what
most dashboards do:

- **A zero draws nothing.** There is no two-pixel stub for "nothing finished that week". Ink
  claiming there was some would be a lie about a bad week.
- **Colour is never the only channel.** Every chart has a legend where it has two series, labels
  on the line ends, and **the same numbers as a table** one click below it. The table is the
  answer to *I do not trust this picture*, and to a screen reader.
- **One axis, always.** Two measures of different scale get two charts, never a second y-axis.

The two chart colours were validated rather than chosen — for lightness band, chroma, separation
under protanopia and deuteranopia at full severity, and contrast against the surface they sit
on. Dark mode has its own steps, checked against the dark surface, rather than an automatic flip
of the light ones.

## What is deliberately not here

- **Nothing across projects.** That is [the portfolio roadmap](/planning/portfolio/), a
  different screen answering a different question.
- **No forecasting.** The burn-up stops at today rather than drawing a flat line into the
  future, because a flat line into the future looks like a prediction and this is a record of
  what happened.
- **No money.** These charts count work, not spend. Money has [screens of its
  own](/planning/budgets/), and the two are not merged: one chart carrying tasks finished
  and euros spent would share an axis between two quantities that have no ratio. There are
  no hourly rates, which is what would connect them.
- **No export button.** The table under each chart is copyable, and every underlying row is
  available over the REST API.
