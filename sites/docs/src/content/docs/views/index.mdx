A project's **Tasks** tab has five layouts, and they are five ways of drawing one set of tasks
rather than five different screens. Switching between them keeps your filters, your grouping and
your selection.

## List

Rows under group headers, with an add button at the end of every group. This is the layout for
reading — the most tasks per screen, the least chrome.

Dragging a row onto another makes it a sub-task. Dragging between groups writes whatever the
grouping is.

## Board

Kanban. Columns are whatever you grouped by, which is your workflow states unless you changed it.
Cards carry assignee, priority, labels, estimate and due date, and dragging one writes the value
of the column you dropped it in.

The **+ column** at the right-hand end adds a workflow state without going into settings — which
is how most teams end up with the *Blocked* or *Review* column they discover they wanted in
week one.

A column can carry a **work-in-progress limit**. It does not refuse the drop; it turns the
column's header when you are over it, which is the useful half of a WIP limit without the half
that makes people work around it.

## Table

A spreadsheet. Sortable columns, every field including custom ones, and inline editing. This is
the layout for auditing — *which of these have no estimate*, *which have been in progress for
three weeks* — and the one to copy out of when somebody wants the numbers in a spreadsheet.

## Calendar

A month grid, drawn from **due dates**. Tasks with no due date are not on it at all — there is
nothing to draw. Dragging a task to another day moves its deadline.

If you want what is due in a calendar you already have open rather than in this one, there is a
[subscribable feed](/beyond/calendar/).

## Timeline

A Gantt: bars from start and due dates, with the blocking relations drawn between them. Dragging a
bar moves everything it blocks.

It is a **layout**, not a separate screen, which is the thing worth knowing about it — the filter
you set on the board still applies here, so "the timeline of everything urgent" is one click rather
than a second set of controls. A task with no dates is not on it, and the count of those is
printed under the chart rather than left for you to notice.

[The timeline](/planning/timeline/) has the rest: working days, lag on a dependency, and
baselines.

## The one that surprises people

**Grouping is not a property of the layout.** Group by assignee and switch to Board, and the
columns are people. Group by a *Select* custom field and the columns are its options. Group by
label, priority, cycle or project and the same thing happens.

That is what makes the board more useful than it looks: a "who is overloaded" board is the same
board grouped differently, and a drag on it hands work over. See
[group, filter, sort](/views/filtering/).

## Where else these show up

The five layouts are the project's. Three other screens draw work in their own shapes:

| | |
|---|---|
| **My work** | Everything on you, across every project in the workspace |
| **Team planner** | A row per person, over a window of days. Dragging a task between rows hands it over |
| **Portfolio** | A roadmap across every project at once — see [across projects](/planning/portfolio/) |
