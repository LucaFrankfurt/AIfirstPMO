This assumes somebody has handed you a URL and a password, or that you have
[started an instance](/start/instance/) yourself. If you would rather poke at a workspace that
already has work in it, [demo.kolibri.day](https://demo.kolibri.day) is exactly that.

## The short version

<Steps>

1. **Sign in.** The first account created on a fresh instance owns it. If you were invited,
   your link creates the account and drops you into the workspace that invited you.

2. **Take the tour, or dismiss it.** A first-run tour sets a few things up as it goes — your
   name, a theme, whether you want email. It is skippable and everything in it is in
   Settings afterwards.

3. **Make a project.** *Projects → New project* in the sidebar. A project needs a name and a
   **key** — two to five letters that become the prefix of every task in it, so `WEB` gives you
   `WEB-1`, `WEB-2`. Pick something you will not mind typing; the key shows up in chat, in
   commit messages and in search.

4. **Put ten real things in it.** Not five perfect ones. Press <kbd>C</kbd> and type — the whole
   task fits on one line:

   ```text
   Redraw the empty state !high @ada #WEB *design due:friday
   ```

   That is a title, a priority, an assignee, a project, a label and a due date. The box shows
   what it understood as chips underneath before you commit to it. See
   [making a task](/tasks/).

5. **Move them.** Switch the project to **Board** and drag. The columns are your workflow states,
   and the **+** at the right-hand end adds one — most teams find they want a *Blocked* or a
   *Review* within the first week.

6. **Start a cycle.** *Project → Cycles → New cycle*. Give it two weeks and drag in what you
   actually mean to finish. The point of the cycle is the line it draws around a promise;
   everything else it does — progress, burn-up, the *Insights* tab — follows from that line
   existing. See [cycles](/planning/cycles/).

7. **Write one page.** *Pages → New page*. The decision you just argued about for forty minutes
   is worth six sentences, and a page is where a task's description stops being the right place
   for it. See [writing pages](/pages/).

8. **Invite the others.** *Settings → Members → Invite*. Roles are owner, admin, member and
   guest; [members and roles](/people/members/) says what each may do.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip" title="Turn signup off once everyone is in">
An instance that lets anybody create an account is an instance that will eventually have
accounts you did not create. Once your team is invited, `KOLIBRI_ALLOW_SIGNUP=false` closes the
door and invites still work. Whoever runs the instance sets that.
</Aside>

## What to skip on the first day

Half of this app is worth meeting only when you feel the need for it. In rough order of when
that usually happens:

| Leave until | |
|---|---|
| Week one | [Saved views](/views/saved/) — the moment you find yourself setting the same three filters twice a day |
| Week one | [Notification settings](/people/notifications/) — the moment the inbox gets noisy |
| Week two | [Modules](/planning/modules/), if the work has milestones that outlive a cycle |
| Week two | [Custom fields](/tasks/custom-fields/), when a label is clearly being used to store an answer rather than a topic |
| Week three | [Rules](/automation/rules/), when you notice a thing somebody has to remember to do every time |
| When it hurts | [The timeline](/planning/timeline/), which earns its keep on work with real dependencies and is overhead on work without them |

## What to do on day two

**Check your notification settings before somebody else does.** The defaults are conservative —
everything lands in the in-app inbox, and email is a batched summary rather than one message per
event — but *everything* still means everything, and how much of it reaches your phone is a
per-person setting. [Notifications](/people/notifications/).

**Turn on two-factor authentication.** *Settings → Profile → Two-factor.* It gives you recovery
codes; put them somewhere that is not the laptop you are sitting at.

**Install it.** Kolibri is a progressive web app — on a phone, *Add to Home Screen*; on a
desktop browser, the install button in the address bar. That is what makes the offline half
useful rather than theoretical. [On your phone](/beyond/mobile/).
